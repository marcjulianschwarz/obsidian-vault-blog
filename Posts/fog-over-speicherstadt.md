Heavy fog sat over Hamburg all weekend. No camera with me, just a phone, which turned out not to matter much.

Fog does the work that a photographer normally has to do by hand. It separates the foreground from everything behind it, kills every distraction past about fifty metres, and flattens the sky into a blank backdrop.

![Brick warehouse with illuminated lettering](/photos/sand-thor-quai-hof.png)

The lit lettering was the only warm thing on the whole street. Against the grey it reads almost like a sign in a film.

## Brick and steel

A few streets further the fog had thickened. The bridge arch and the Speicherstadt facade behind it were the only shapes left with any edge to them.

![Bridge arch in front of a brick facade](/photos/hafen-city-bridge.png)

Everything past the building is just gone. On a clear day this frame has cranes, cables, and half the harbour in it, and it is a much worse picture.

Shooting a phone in this light has one rule worth following: turn off every automatic enhancement you can. The processing wants to pull detail out of the fog and add contrast back, which is precisely the thing you came out for.
